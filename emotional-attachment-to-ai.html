Emotional attachment to AI

10 questions about whether AI has become a source of comfort and company for you. Answer about the last month — about two minutes. Your answers stay in your browser.

1. Do you turn to AI when you feel lonely?
2. Do you tell AI about what worries you before you tell anyone else?
3. Do you talk to AI to calm down when you are upset?
4. Do you choose to talk to AI instead of meeting or calling people?
5. Do you spend less time with friends or family because you are talking to AI?
6. Do you feel that AI understands you better than the people around you?
7. Do you think of an AI chatbot as a friend or companion?
8. Do you miss talking to AI when you have not used it for a day?
9. Do you care about what an AI chatbot “thinks” of you?
10. Do you look forward to talking to AI more than to talking to people?

Please answer all 10 questions.

Your 7-day plan

For “AI as comfort” — the area where you scored highest. Small steps, no bans.

  1. Days 1–2. When you feel lonely or upset, first write down what you feel, and open the chat only after that.
  2. Days 3–5. Make a short list of things that calm you without a screen: a walk, music, a shower, tea.
  3. Days 6–7. Once this week, tell a person, not AI, about something that worries you.

Your 7-day plan

For “Bond with the chatbot” — the area where you scored highest. Small steps, no bans.

  2. Days 3–5. Try one day this week without talking to the chatbot and see how it goes.

Main test: how much do you rely on AI? 📐 Methodology

About this test

This test helps you notice whether AI has become your main way to feel better or a stand-in for the people close to you. Talking to a chatbot is fine; the test only shows how big a place it has taken in your life. It is not a medical test and not a diagnosis. Your level is counted only from your own answers, not by comparing you with other people.

More: the research behind the test and how the score is counted

The questions are our own. They follow the outcomes of a four-week randomized study of 981 chatbot users by MIT Media Lab and OpenAI researchers (Fang, Liu et al., 2025, arXiv:2503.17473): those who chose to use the chatbot more, and those who trusted it more and felt socially drawn to it, reported more loneliness and emotional dependence and less time with people — associations, not proven causes. They also follow the mood-modification area of the Conversational AI Dependence Scale (Chen et al., 2025). Each answer gives 0–4 points, the total is 0–40; levels: up to 40% of the maximum, 41–70%, above 70% — thresholds from the answer scale, not population norms. The test has not been clinically validated and is not a diagnosis.
