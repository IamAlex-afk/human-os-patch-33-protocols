What science knows about AI and our minds

Research on AI and the mind is young. Here is what studies have found so far, in plain words, with how strong the evidence is and a link to each study. Checked on 21 October 2026.

What is “cognitive offloading”?

It is handing a mental task to something outside your head — a note, a search engine, an AI assistant. People have always done it; it saves effort, but it changes what we practise ourselves.

Evidence: Overview of many studies · Study: Risko & Gilbert, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2016

Do we remember less when we can look things up?

In experiments, people who expected to find information later remembered where to find it better than the information itself.

Evidence: Series of experiments · Study: Sparrow, Liu & Wegner, Science, 2011

Does trust in AI change how much we think?

In a survey of 319 knowledge workers, those who trusted generative AI more thought less critically about its answers, and those who trusted themselves more thought more.

Evidence: Survey: shows a link, not a cause · Study: Lee et al., CHI 2025

Is heavy AI use linked to weaker critical thinking?

In a study of 666 people, those who used AI tools more often had lower critical-thinking scores. The two go together; the study does not show that AI is the cause.

Evidence: Survey: shows a link, not a cause · Study: Gerlich, Societies, 2025

What happens in the brain when AI writes with us?

In an experiment with 54 people writing essays, the group using an AI assistant had the weakest links between brain areas of three groups. It is a small early study that other scientists have not yet checked.

Evidence: Small early study, not yet checked by other scientists · Study: Kosmyna et al., arXiv:2506.08872

Can a chatbot make people lonelier?

In a four-week experiment with 981 people by MIT Media Lab and OpenAI, those who used the chatbot more each day tended to feel lonelier, meet people less and depend on it more emotionally. These are links, not proof of cause.

Evidence: Experiment with people over 4 weeks · Study: Fang, Liu et al., arXiv:2503.17473

Is “AI addiction” a diagnosis?

No, it is not an official diagnosis. Researchers are building questionnaires to measure dependence on AI; for example, two scales published in 2025 look at the pull towards AI, losing control and how people feel without it.

Evidence: Research questionnaires · Study: Goh, Hartanto & Majeed 2025; Chen et al. 2025

Is fear of AI studied?

Yes. The AI Anxiety Scale divides it into four parts: learning AI, losing jobs, not seeing that AI depends on people, and AI that looks or acts human.

Evidence: Research questionnaires · Study: Wang & Wang, Interactive Learning Environments

Do experts and the public see AI the same way?

No. In 2024 polls in the US, 17% of adults but 56% of AI experts expected AI to have a positive effect on the country over 20 years; 51% of adults and 15% of experts were more worried than excited.

Evidence: Large opinion poll · Study: Pew Research Center, 2025

What follows from this: none of these studies proves that AI makes people less smart or ill. They show that how we use AI matters — and that it is worth keeping some thinking, memory and company for ourselves. Our tests help you look at your own habits.
