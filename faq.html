🧠 What is AI? Simple answers

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a computer system that can do tasks that normally require human intelligence — like understanding language, recognizing images, making decisions, and learning from experience. Modern AI learns from large amounts of data.
Source: Wikipedia

The term 'artificial intelligence' was coined in 1956 at the Dartmouth Conference, but ideas of thinking machines go back to antiquity. Massive development started in the 2010s with more computing power and data.
Source: Stanford Encyclopedia

Modern AI uses machine learning: it analyzes millions of examples, finds patterns, and builds mathematical models.
Source: IBM

Three levels are usually cited: Narrow AI (solves one task, like ChatGPT), General AI (AGI — hypothetical, human-level), and Super AI (exceeds humans, doesn't exist yet).
Source: Wikipedia

Like any technology, AI brings risks: uncontrolled use, spreading misinformation, job losses. But with responsible approach, AI remains a powerful tool that helps people.
Source: Future of Life Institute

“Brain rot” is slang, not a diagnosis. Handing mental work to a tool has a scientific name: cognitive offloading (Risko & Gilbert, 2016). In one small study, people who wrote essays with an AI chatbot showed the weakest brain connectivity of three groups (Kosmyna et al., 2025, 54 people, not yet peer-reviewed). That is an early signal, not proof of damage.

Often it is a matter of practice: what you always hand to AI, you practise less. In a survey of 319 knowledge workers, those who trusted generative AI more reported less critical thinking, and those more confident in their own skills reported more (Lee et al., 2025). You don't need to quit: start by doing one small task without AI.
