📋 What this assessment measures

These 10 questions look at three signs that AI dependency researchers describe: a pull towards AI, losing control over how much you use it, and how you feel without it. It is a way to look at yourself, not a medical diagnosis. The questions are our own (sources below).

Who it's for: Anyone who wants to check whether their AI use is still intentional or becoming automatic. You get a score from 0 to 40, a level in words and one small next step.

How it works: For each question, choose how often it applies to you over the last month. Everything runs inside your browser — no data leaves your device.

Sources: we wrote the questions ourselves, based on these studies. Our test is not one of these research questionnaires and has not been tested on people the way they were.

AI dependency

Answer about the last month. 10 questions, about two minutes; your answers stay in your browser.

1. Do you think about using AI even when you are not using it?
2. Do you open AI out of habit, before you know whether you need it?
3. Do you spend more time with AI than you planned?
4. Have you tried to use AI less and not managed to?
5. Do you put off your own tasks because you are busy with AI?
6. Has your use of AI caused problems at school, at work or with people close to you?
7. Do you feel you can no longer do things you used to do without AI?
8. Do you feel irritable or restless when you cannot use AI?
9. Do you feel lost when AI is unavailable for a while?
10. Do you turn to AI to feel better when you are stressed or down?

Please answer all 10 questions.

  2. Days 3–5. Do one task you have been putting off first, and open AI only after it.
  3. Days 6–7. At the end of the week, write down where AI really helped and where it took your time.

Your 7-day plan

For “Without AI and mood” — the area where you scored highest. Small steps, no bans.

  1. Days 1–2. When you feel restless without AI, take five slow breaths and wait two minutes.
  2. Days 3–5. Each day, do one thing you used to do without AI: a short text, a calculation, a plan.
  3. Days 6–7. When you feel low, first try talking to a person or going for a walk, and turn to AI only after that.

In one or two weeks, take the test again and compare.

“Brain fog” is not a medical diagnosis, and science has not shown that AI causes it. AIAS, the AI Anxiety Scale, is a questionnaire developed by Wang and Wang (Wang & Wang, 2019). It measures anxiety about AI in four areas: learning about AI, job replacement, sociotechnical blindness (not seeing that AI depends on people) and AI configuration (unease about how AI looks and behaves).

AIRD is not an official diagnosis but an idea proposed by researchers: a person worries so much that AI will replace them at work that it gets hard to cope with everyday life. It was described by University of Florida researchers in the medical journal Cureus.
